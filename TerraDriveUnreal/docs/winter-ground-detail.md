# Snow and meadow detail

The native proving ground now uses project-owned copies of the acquired Winter Scene and MW ground scans, with zero texture LOD bias and a 2K size ceiling. Only these bounded ground copies stay resident, because the custom world-position sampling does not provide a reliable UV0 streaming scale. The scenery library retains its normal streaming. Original acquired textures remain unchanged. The Winter Scene's clean-snow, crushed snow-road and pebble maps supply color, normal detail and roughness; meadow grass uses finer MW texture scale and more varied planted clumps. The seven existing terrain coverage fields still control the natural fringes.

The custom glTF scan reader now compensates for Unreal's OBJ V-coordinate flip. Without that conversion, some mossy rocks sampled the empty black part of their texture atlas. The change is limited to the 37 generated CC0 scan meshes: their positions, normals, faces, material slots and placements remain identical, as do vehicle and course UVs and the original textures. The source mapping is checked offline, and the final cooked-game captures show corrected grey/tan rock detail, varied meadow texture and irregular snow fringes. Fine snow contrast remains subtle, and a foreground plant partly obscures the wide drift view; the visual record retains those limitations.

The scan-grass recipe adds the original [Poly Haven grass opacity map](https://polyhaven.com/a/grass_medium_01), acquired under CC0, to its pinned offline sources. Its separate grayscale mask uses the red channel and a masked cutout at 0.5; the RGB JPEG supplied with the glTF has no opacity channel. This affects the scan-grass material only. The acquired MW grass materials and the other scan materials keep their existing shading.

Snow has wind-shaped physical relief above the preserved source height field. The relief is sampled at the same one-metre vertices used by the coarse rendered terrain and chassis collision, then inherited by the finer deformable tire tiles. Its nested wind waves span roughly **12–120 feet**, giving visible shelves and ripples within the basin instead of a single nearly basin-wide undulation. The relief adds approximately **3–24 inches** above the original field in the snow interior, fading with snow coverage at the fringes. The original terrain.bin and browser terrain remain recovery references. Tire grooves, compaction and raised shoulders continue to operate relative to this new snow base.

The ground shader retains snow scan detail, blue-grey hollows, granular normal detail and broken crushed-snow patches. It preserves scanned roughness, scales bright albedo without clipping its detail, and uses the material shared anisotropic sampler for shallow viewing angles. The snow-road strip keeps its 1:4 texture aspect. Mixed snow fringes develop darker, smoother slush rather than a uniform white fade. Three acquired snow-chunk meshes add irregular banks around the basin. Those decorative snow clumps do not act as concrete obstacles; the underlying native relief supplies the physical surface. MW grass clumps retain crushing and have bounded view distances without shadow passes. Original crushable summer ground-cover cards are suppressed where snow coverage exceeds 65%; their instance indices are preserved for the existing crushing and recovery bookkeeping. Other authored vegetation remains.

The separate [complete Winter Expedition](winter-level.md) retains Epic's authored landscape, road, cliffs, foliage and lighting. It uses the authored scene collision and original landscape materials. Deformable native ruts are supported in the proving ground; digging below the separate sample landscape is deliberately disabled because an overlay would otherwise disappear under the original landscape. Full landscape deformation in that destination requires a separate render/collision integration.

Run `Tools/verify-winter.ps1 -Mode Detail` for scan-resolution checks, physical drift variation, snowbank/meadow counts and six native ground views. `-Mode Winter` performs real travel into the complete authored environment and back. Accepted native results and screenshot hashes belong in [native-validation.json](native-validation.json); source recipes alone are not native visual evidence.
