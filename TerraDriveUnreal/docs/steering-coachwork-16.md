## Tire contact and steering

The old calculation assigned sideways tire force first, then gave longitudinal contact only the unused friction ellipse. At saturated sideways grip the remaining rolling reaction became zero. Rolling resistance could then hold a freely moving tire at zero RPM. The new implicit wheel and locked-axle solvers resolve longitudinal and lateral slip together within the same bounded friction ellipse. Brake holding is still solved with the tire reaction, so genuine locked-wheel braking remains possible. Individual Ackermann angles align inside/outside wheels with the same turn centre, including the existing rear-steer vehicles.

`Tools/test-core.cmd` exercises forward/reverse coasting at zero, partial and saturated lateral demand, locked axles, actual brake locking, torque balance and bounded friction at 10/20/30/60 Hz. `Tools/verify-turning.ps1` exercises all nine native vehicles in forward/reverse and left/right full-lock coasts, plus locked front/rear axles on the Wrangler and G63. It checks moving loaded contacts, continuous angular motion, actual mesh/rim pose and retained chassis movement. A controlled initial rolling speed is seeded once; the chassis and tires then coast under the actual solver. It does not substitute wheel animation for measured contact motion.

Native regression also exposed slope creep under the service brake when cross-axle articulation unloaded tires. Gravity compensation used four nominal tire shares, leaving some support assigned to lifted wheels. It now follows the valid tires actually carrying load, with the existing brake-torque, contact-inertia and friction-ellipse bounds. Thirty-four additional portable incline cases require a hold with adequate grip, continued sliding with inadequate grip, bounded friction, and no artificial support from unloaded or airborne tires. The simulation core now passes 648 checks; current native acceptance is recorded separately.
